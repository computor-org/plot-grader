## Conclusions

- **The official tests are wrong about a third of the time against the
  criteria (0.68).** They fail 48 acceptable plots (upper-case title 10/10,
  curves drawn in another order 9/10, halved sampling 4/5, old assignment
  copies with a different title) and pass 29 wrong ones (log y axis 13/14,
  halved x range 12/15). Tier 1 is stricter still (0.57).
- **The prompt decides the tolerance, not the model.** The same Flash-Next
  goes from 0.67 (strict prompt, 77 false fails) to 0.89 (tolerant prompt,
  1 false fail). It passes 47 of the 48 acceptable plots the tests fail and
  is stable (239/244 identical verdicts over two runs).
- **The tolerant model is too tolerant for missing axis labels and scaled
  data:** it passes 11 of 13 plots without axis labels and 6 of 14 with
  data scaled by 25 %. It catches log axes, cut x ranges and missing curves.
- **Tolerant data checks close exactly that gap.** `src/plot_grader/tolerant.py`
  checks only content: panels, that labelled axes stay labelled (any
  wording), curve values in any order and sampling (5 % of the y range) and
  the visible x range. Model and checks together: 0.93, 3 false passes.
  10 of its 13 false fails are the unseeded Monte-Carlo exercise, where a
  new random draw cannot match the reference data (criterion T6); such
  exercises need the model alone or a fixed seed in the task.
- **Pixel identity is a safe shortcut:** 54 of 55 pixel-identical plots are
  acceptable.
- **Chat, not JEV:** JEV with the single tolerant question is as accurate
  (0.88) but 2.4× slower here (13.7 vs 33 calls/min).
